# Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act in K–12 Schools

**Date checked:** November 12, 2026
**Scope:** Federal Section 504 requirements most relevant to public elementary and secondary education. This page distinguishes binding regulation from nonbinding agency guidance. It is informational, not legal advice and not an automatic disability, FAPE, accommodation, or discrimination determination.

## What Section 504 is

Section 504 is a federal civil-rights law that prohibits disability discrimination by programs or activities receiving federal financial assistance. Education regulations for preschool, elementary, and secondary programs are in **34 C.F.R. Part 104, Subpart D**. Public school districts that receive federal financial assistance generally must comply with the applicable requirements.

- Statutory text, 29 U.S.C. § 794: [Rehabilitation Act § 504](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title29/html/USCODE-2024-title29-chap16-subchapV-sec794.htm)
- Binding regulations: [34 C.F.R. Part 104](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/part-104)
- Definitions: [34 C.F.R. § 104.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-104.3)

## Key K–12 requirements

### Nondiscrimination and equal opportunity

A qualified person with a disability may not, solely because of disability, be excluded from participation in, denied the benefits of, or subjected to discrimination under a covered education program. Schools must provide students with disabilities an equal opportunity to participate in academic, nonacademic, and extracurricular activities.

- General prohibition: [34 C.F.R. § 104.4](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-104.4)
- Nonacademic services and activities: [34 C.F.R. § 104.37](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-104.37)

### FAPE under Section 504

For public elementary and secondary education, a recipient must provide a free appropriate public education to each qualified person with a disability in its jurisdiction, regardless of the nature or severity of the disability. Section 504 FAPE is defined as regular or special education and related aids and services designed to meet the individual educational needs of students with disabilities as adequately as the needs of nondisabled students are met. Services may be provided through an IEP, a Section 504 plan, or another documented system that satisfies the regulation.

- FAPE: [34 C.F.R. § 104.33](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-104.33)
- Educational setting/LRE: [34 C.F.R. § 104.34](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-104.34)

### Evaluation and placement procedures

Section 504 requires evaluation before initial placement and before any subsequent significant change in placement, using procedures that satisfy the regulation. The evaluation must draw on information from a variety of sources and must be documented and carefully considered. Placement decisions must be made by a group of persons knowledgeable about the student, evaluation data, and placement options, and must be periodically reevaluated.

- Evaluation and placement: [34 C.F.R. § 104.35](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-104.35)
- Procedural safeguards: [34 C.F.R. § 104.36](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-104.36)

### Reasonable modifications and aids

A school may need to provide related aids, services, and reasonable modifications so a qualified student can access education. The support must be individualized and tied to documented educational needs. A school should not assume that a diagnosis automatically dictates a particular accommodation, and a teacher should not independently add, remove, or alter a plan outside the school’s established process.

## Section 504 plan compared with an IEP

An IEP is developed under IDEA for a student who meets IDEA eligibility requirements and needs special education and related services. A Section 504 plan is one way a school may document the regular or special education and related aids and services needed under Section 504; Section 504 does not require a specific document called an “IAP” or “504 plan” in the federal regulation. IDEA-eligible children with disabilities are protected by Section 504; not every student protected by Section 504 is eligible under IDEA. [Department of Education explanation](https://www.ed.gov/laws-and-policy/civil-rights-laws/disability-discrimination/frequently-asked-questions-disability-discrimination).

A school should analyze the actual facts, evaluations, educational needs, and applicable state/local procedures. Do not use this page to convert a diagnosis, medical note, or classroom difficulty directly into a legal conclusion.

## Guidance versus law

The eCFR provisions cited above are binding regulations. OCR “Dear Colleague” letters, FAQs, and technical-assistance materials explain the Department’s position and may be useful for implementation, but guidance is not a substitute for the statute or regulations and generally does not itself create new legal obligations.

- OCR disability-discrimination technical assistance and FAQs: [U.S. Department of Education Office for Civil Rights](https://www.ed.gov/laws-and-policy/civil-rights-laws)
- OCR charter-school Section 504 FAQ (expressly identified as nonbinding significant guidance): [PDF](https://www2.ed.gov/about/offices/list/ocr/docs/dcl-faq-201612-504-charter-school.pdf)
- OCR hidden-disabilities publication: [The Civil Rights of Students With Hidden Disabilities and Section 504](https://www2.ed.gov/about/offices/list/ocr/docs/hq5269.html)

## Practical implementation cautions

- Provide staff with the implementation information they need, while limiting disclosure to legitimate educational need and following FERPA and local confidentiality rules.
- Document what was provided, when, and any observable access or participation issue; avoid diagnostic speculation in classroom notes.
- Refer suspected unmet needs, changed circumstances, or disputes to the designated Section 504 coordinator or appropriate team.
- Check current state assessment rules separately. A classroom accommodation is not automatically permitted on every state or federal assessment.

## Limits

Section 504 is fact-specific. This page does not determine whether a student is a “qualified person with a disability,” whether a particular aid is reasonable, whether FAPE was provided, or whether discrimination occurred. Those are determinations for the responsible school/district process, OCR, or a court or hearing body as applicable.
