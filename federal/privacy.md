# Student Privacy for IDEA, Section 504, and Classroom Support

**Date checked:** November 12, 2026
**Scope:** Federal privacy rules most relevant to public-school education records, IDEA records, Section 504 implementation, and deidentified knowledgebase work. This page is general information, not legal advice or a school-specific privacy determination.

## Privacy baseline

The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) generally protects personally identifiable information (PII) from education records maintained by an educational agency or institution that receives funds under a program administered by the U.S. Department of Education. FERPA gives parents rights to inspect and review records, request amendment of records, and consent to disclosures of PII, subject to statutory and regulatory exceptions. Rights generally transfer to the student at age 18 or attendance at a postsecondary institution (an “eligible student”).

- U.S. Department of Education FERPA overview: [Student Privacy—FERPA](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/ferpa)
- FERPA regulations: [34 C.F.R. Part 99](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/part-99)
- Access and amendment rights: [34 C.F.R. §§ 99.10–99.21](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/part-99/subpart-B)
- Consent before disclosure: [34 C.F.R. § 99.30](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.30)

## Common disclosure rules

A school generally must obtain written consent before disclosing PII from an education record unless a FERPA exception applies. Common exceptions include disclosures to school officials with legitimate educational interests, certain contractors or parties performing institutional services under required controls, disclosures connected with a transfer to another school, directory-information disclosures under the school’s required notice and opt-out process, and a health-or-safety-emergency exception under specified conditions.

- Exceptions to consent: [34 C.F.R. § 99.31](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.31)
- Record of disclosures: [34 C.F.R. § 99.32](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.32)
- School-official exception: [34 C.F.R. § 99.31(a)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.31#a-1)
- Directory information and opt-out notice: [34 C.F.R. § 99.37](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.37)
- Health-or-safety emergency: [34 C.F.R. § 99.36](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.36)

FERPA exceptions are permissions, not blanket mandates. A school must still follow the conditions of the applicable exception, its annual notices, state law, contracts, and local policy. For an emergency disclosure, the school must make the required case-by-case determination and record the threat and parties receiving information as required by the regulation.

- Department FAQ on health-or-safety emergencies: [When is it permissible to use FERPA’s health or safety emergency exception?](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/when-it-permissible-utilize-ferpas-health-or-safety-emergency-exception-disclosures)
- Department FAQ on school officials: [May a school disclose records to any employee?](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/under-ferpa-may-educational-agency-or-institution-disclose-education-records-any-its-employees)

## IDEA records and confidentiality

IDEA has additional confidentiality-of-information requirements for personally identifiable information collected, maintained, or used under IDEA. The IDEA confidentiality rules generally incorporate FERPA protections and address notice, access, amendment, consent, destruction, and safeguards. A school should apply both IDEA requirements and FERPA requirements when handling special-education records.

- IDEA confidentiality regulations: [34 C.F.R. §§ 300.610–300.625](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/f/)
- IDEA access to records: [34 C.F.R. § 300.613](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.613)
- IDEA consent: [34 C.F.R. § 300.622](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.622)

Section 504 does not replace FERPA. A Section 504 plan, evaluation, medical information supplied to the school, and implementation records may be subject to FERPA depending on whether they are education records maintained by the school. Handle them through the district’s designated process and access controls.

## Deidentified knowledgebase rules

This repository’s content must be public educational material or wholly fictional examples, not deidentified real student records. Do not include:

- student names, initials, IDs, birth dates, addresses, contact details, or exact school/classroom identifiers;
- photographs, audio, copied IEPs, 504 plans, evaluation reports, medical records, or test-session artifacts;
- combinations of facts that could reasonably re-identify a student, even if a name is removed;
- private credentials, account identifiers, or staff-only case notes.

Use hypothetical examples with altered, non-identifying facts. Do not submit real student scenarios or source materials to this repository, even after removing names. Deidentification is a risk-reduction practice; it does not automatically resolve every FERPA, state-law, contractual, or professional-confidentiality issue.

## Operational checklist

1. Identify whether the material is a student record or contains PII.
2. If yes or uncertain, do not add it to a public or broadly shared knowledgebase.
3. Prefer official public statutes, regulations, and agency guidance over copied student documents.
4. Share only the minimum information needed for a legitimate educational purpose.
5. Confirm whether consent, a documented exception, or an authorized school-official role applies before disclosure.
6. Keep an audit trail for permitted disclosures where required.
7. Escalate uncertain cases to the district privacy officer, FERPA/504/IDEA coordinator, or counsel.

## Limits

This page does not classify a particular record, authorize a disclosure, or determine whether a deidentification method is sufficient. FERPA applies to education records, not every piece of information a school employee may possess, and other federal, state, contractual, and ethical obligations may apply. When facts are close or sensitive, stop and seek the responsible district review.
