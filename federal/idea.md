# Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA)

**Date checked:** November 12, 2026
**Scope:** U.S. federal IDEA Part B concepts relevant to public-school students ages 3–21, with implementation notes for educators and families. This is an informational knowledgebase page, not legal advice and not an eligibility, placement, or compliance determination.

## What IDEA is

The IDEA is a federal special-education statute. Part B authorizes federal assistance to states and establishes requirements for a **free appropriate public education (FAPE)** for eligible children with disabilities. IDEA implementation is carried out through federal regulations, state rules, and local procedures; the statute and regulations control over this summary.

- IDEA statute and regulations index: [U.S. Department of Education IDEA site](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/statuteregulations/)
- Part B regulations, 34 C.F.R. Part 300: [eCFR Part 300](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-B/chapter-III/part-300)
- FAPE and state eligibility requirements: [20 U.S.C. § 1412](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/statute-chapter-33/subchapter-ii/1412/)

## Core concepts

### Child find and evaluation

Public agencies must have policies and procedures to identify, locate, and evaluate children with disabilities who may need special education and related services, including children advancing from grade to grade. A parent, teacher, or other source may refer a child for an initial evaluation under applicable procedures. Screening or classroom intervention alone is not an IDEA evaluation.

- Child find: [34 C.F.R. § 300.111](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.111)
- Initial evaluation and timeline: [34 C.F.R. §§ 300.301–300.306](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/d/)
- Evaluation procedures: [34 C.F.R. § 300.304](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.304)

### Eligibility and services

Eligibility is determined under IDEA disability categories and state-adopted criteria, using a comprehensive evaluation and a group decision that includes qualified professionals and the parent. A medical diagnosis, by itself, does not automatically establish IDEA eligibility; likewise, poor grades alone do not resolve eligibility. The team must consider whether the child has a qualifying disability and, because of it, needs special education and related services.

- Eligibility determination: [34 C.F.R. § 300.306](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.306)
- Definition of child with a disability: [34 C.F.R. § 300.8](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.8)
- Specific learning disability provisions: [34 C.F.R. §§ 300.307–300.311](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/d/)

### IEP

An individualized education program (IEP) is the written plan for a child who is eligible under IDEA. It includes measurable annual goals, how progress will be measured and reported, special education and related services, supplementary aids and services, program modifications or supports for school personnel, participation in assessments, and the projected start, frequency, location, and duration of services as required by the regulation.

The IEP team includes the parent, at least one regular-education teacher when applicable, at least one special-education teacher or provider, a public-agency representative, someone able to interpret evaluation results, and others with relevant knowledge when invited. The IEP is reviewed at least annually and revised when needed.

- IEP content: [34 C.F.R. § 300.320](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.320)
- IEP team: [34 C.F.R. § 300.321](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.321)
- Parent participation: [34 C.F.R. § 300.322](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.322)
- IEP development and annual review: [34 C.F.R. § 300.324](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.324)

### Least restrictive environment (LRE)

To the maximum extent appropriate, students with disabilities are educated with students who are not disabled. Removal from the regular educational environment occurs only when education there, with supplementary aids and services, cannot be achieved satisfactorily. Placement is decided by a group including the parents and knowledgeable school personnel, based on the IEP and applicable requirements—not by a single teacher or a diagnosis label.

- LRE and placement: [34 C.F.R. §§ 300.114–300.116](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/b/)

### Procedural safeguards and dispute resolution

IDEA provides procedural safeguards, including prior written notice, parental consent in specified circumstances, access to education records, independent educational evaluations in defined circumstances, mediation, state complaints, due-process hearings, and appeal or civil-action rights subject to applicable timelines. Schools must provide the procedural-safeguards notice at required times and upon parent request.

- Statutory safeguards: [20 U.S.C. § 1415](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/statute-chapter-33/subchapter-ii/1415/)
- Prior written notice: [34 C.F.R. § 300.503](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.503)
- Procedural-safeguards notice: [34 C.F.R. § 300.504](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.504)
- IDEA dispute-resolution regulations: [34 C.F.R. Part 300, Subpart E](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/e/)

## IDEA and classroom practice

An IEP is an enforceable individualized plan within the school’s responsibilities. Staff should be able to access the accommodations, modifications, services, supports, and implementation details they need for their assigned duties, while protecting student privacy. Classroom accommodations should be implemented as written and documented factually; concerns about feasibility, missing information, or lack of progress should be routed to the case manager or IEP team rather than unilaterally changing the plan.

Examples of IEP supports may include accessible instructional materials, explicit instruction, assistive technology, extended time when specified, breaks, preferential seating, visual supports, behavior supports, adapted response methods, or related services. The appropriate support depends on the student’s documented needs and the IEP—not on a generic disability category.

## Important limits

- IDEA does not require a particular diagnosis, commercial program, classroom strategy, or placement in every case.
- IDEA and Section 504 are related but distinct laws; a student may qualify under one, both, or neither.
- State and local procedures may add requirements. For Texas, consult the district’s ARD/IEP procedures and current Texas Education Agency materials.
- This page does not decide whether a student is eligible, whether an IEP is adequate, or whether a school has complied with IDEA. Those questions require review of the facts, governing law, and applicable administrative process.
