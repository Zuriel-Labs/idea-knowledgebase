# Practical Classroom Accommodations Guidance

**Date checked:** November 12, 2026
**Scope:** Practical, non-exhaustive guidance for teachers and school staff supporting students with documented IDEA or Section 504 plans. It includes Texas/STAAR implementation cautions because state testing rules are separate from federal law. It is not a menu that creates an entitlement, and it does not make automatic legal or eligibility determinations.

## Start with the student’s plan

Use the current IEP, Section 504 plan, or other authorized school documentation as the source of truth. Identify the exact accommodation, setting, frequency, duration, materials, response mode, and assessment limits. A diagnosis, parent request, or generic list cannot substitute for the team’s individualized decision.

If the plan is unclear, contradictory, impractical, or appears not to address a changed need, do not silently rewrite it. Record the operational question and contact the case manager, Section 504 coordinator, or campus administrator for the school’s established review process.

## Accommodation versus modification

- **Accommodation:** changes access, presentation, response, timing, setting, or organization without changing the learning target being measured.
- **Modification:** changes what the student is expected to learn or demonstrate; it may affect curriculum, grading, or assessment interpretation.
- **Support or service:** may include specially designed instruction, related services, assistive technology, behavior supports, or personnel assistance.

The distinction is context-dependent. Follow the student’s plan and assessment rules rather than labeling a support from a generic chart.

## Implementation workflow

1. **Read and translate the plan into routines.** Write a short staff-facing implementation note using only the information staff need.
2. **Prepare materials and access.** Set up approved formats, devices, visual supports, seating, breaks, or response tools before instruction begins.
3. **Teach the routine.** Ensure the student knows how to request or use the support; do not wait until a high-stakes test.
4. **Use consistently.** Apply the support in the settings and subjects specified, including classroom tests when the plan requires it.
5. **Observe access, not diagnosis.** Record objective facts such as “completed 8 of 10 items with speech-to-text” rather than medical or behavioral speculation.
6. **Check effectiveness.** Share patterns of progress, non-use, frustration, or changed need with the responsible team.
7. **Protect privacy.** Share implementation details only with staff who have a legitimate educational need to know.

## Common support categories and examples

These examples are starting points only. The student’s plan controls.

### Presentation and access

- accessible digital text, large print, Braille, captions, audio, or text-to-speech when specified;
- chunked directions, plain-language restatement that does not reveal answers, visual schedules, models, and highlighted essential information;
- preview of vocabulary or procedures through instruction—not unauthorized assistance on a test item;
- assistive technology and accessible files tested before the lesson.

### Response and output

- speech-to-text, scribe/transcription, keyboarding, pointing, augmentative communication, or alternate response formats when documented;
- graphic organizers, checklists, or word-processing supports when permitted by the plan and assessment rules;
- additional workspace or reduced copying demands when the documented need concerns motor, visual, or written-output access.

### Timing and breaks

- extended time, frequent breaks, or stop-the-clock breaks only as specified;
- a predictable location and return routine for breaks;
- no penalty for disability-related processing or response time when the plan and applicable assessment rules authorize the support.

### Setting and attention

- preferential seating based on the access need, reduced-distraction setting, small-group or individual administration when authorized;
- visual timers, discreet prompts, task check-ins, or structured reminders when documented;
- positive behavioral supports and regulation tools that do not remove instruction or become a substitute for teaching.

### Organization and executive function

- agenda or visual sequence, assignment chunking, interim deadlines, explicit transition warnings, materials checklist, and teacher confirmation of directions;
- one consistent place for posted directions and due dates;
- prompts that fade when possible while preserving the documented support.

## Assessment cautions

A classroom accommodation is not automatically allowed on every assessment. Before STAAR or another state test, consult the current state assessment manual, eligibility criteria, documentation requirements, and test-administrator rules. Never provide help that changes the construct being measured, reveals an answer, paraphrases test content beyond what the rule permits, or violates test security.

### Texas and STAAR

The Texas Education Agency describes accommodations as changes to materials or procedures that enable meaningful participation in learning and testing. TEA separates **accessibility features**, **locally approved designated supports**, and **designated supports requiring TEA approval**. Eligibility and documentation vary by support and assessment. Supports generally must be used routinely and effectively during classroom instruction and classroom testing when the current TEA rule requires that condition; an IEP or 504 plan alone is not a substitute for checking the current STAAR rule.

- TEA accommodations hub: [Accommodations Resources](https://tea.texas.gov/assessment-and-accountability/accommodations-resources)
- TEA current assessment information: [Student Assessment](https://tea.texas.gov/student-assessment)
- Current guide located in the TEA materials: [2026–2027 STAAR Accommodations Educator Guide](https://tea.texas.gov/data-reports/staar/staar-accommodations-educator-guide.pdf)
- For operational decisions, use the current TEA educator guide and district testing coordinator instructions linked from the hub. Older PDFs and archived STAAR materials may be useful history but should not override the current guide.

## Privacy boundary

This public repository must never contain student identifiers, individualized accommodation lists, completed work, plans, or real case notes. A school may document implementation in its authorized private systems under its own policies; a local student ID is identifying information, not anonymous. See [privacy](federal/privacy.md).

## Escalate instead of improvising when

- the accommodation conflicts with test-security rules or the assessment manual;
- staff cannot tell whether a support is an accommodation, modification, service, or assessment restriction;
- the student’s needs have changed materially;
- a required device, interpreter, aide, accessible format, or related service is unavailable;
- staff are being asked to disclose records beyond legitimate educational need;
- implementation failures are recurring or affect access to instruction.

## Boundaries

This guide does not decide eligibility, disability status, FAPE, discrimination, plan compliance, or whether a specific support is allowed on a specific test. Those determinations require the applicable team, current federal/state rules, and documented facts. When in doubt, pause the high-stakes action and ask the designated school or district coordinator.
