# Texas education accommodations: overview

> **Scope and currency.** This is a public-policy knowledgebase for Texas public schools, not legal advice and not a substitute for the student’s current ARD/IEP, Section 504 plan, district procedures, or TEA coordinator instructions. Checked against official sources available **November 12, 2026**. The current testing year is **2026–27**; STAAR remains the end-of-year state assessment for that year. TEA says the Student Success Tool (SST) replaces STAAR beginning in **2027–28**, with certain STAAR end-of-course administrations continuing through December 2027; see [TEA’s transition calendar](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/school-year-2027-28-student-assessment-testing-calendar).

## The basic distinction

- **Classroom accommodations** change how a student accesses instruction, assignments, or demonstrates learning without changing the academic standard being taught. Examples can include accessible presentation, assistive technology, preferential seating, breaks, or extended time. The exact support must be individualized, implemented consistently, and documented in the applicable plan.
- **Modifications** change what a student is expected to learn or the complexity/standard of the work. They are not interchangeable with accommodations and can affect grading, course credit, and assessment interpretation.
- **State-assessment accommodations** are narrower than classroom practice. A support used in class is not automatically allowed on STAAR. TEA categorizes STAAR supports as accessibility features, locally approved designated supports, or designated supports requiring TEA approval.

## Which plan governs?

### IDEA / ARD / IEP

Texas uses an **Admission, Review, and Dismissal (ARD) committee** as the IEP team. Under 19 TAC §89.1050, the ARD committee performs the IEP-team functions required by federal law, including decisions about the student’s IEP, placement, least restrictive environment, and participation in state assessments. The IEP must comply with 34 CFR §§300.320 and 300.324 and Texas Education Code §29.005; Texas rule §89.1055 requires annual review, present levels of academic achievement and functional performance (PLAAFP), measurable goals, and other required components.

The IEP should state supports concretely: setting, frequency/duration, conditions, responsible staff, materials/technology, and how effectiveness will be monitored. “As needed” without operational detail is a weak implementation safeguard.

### Section 504

Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act is a civil-rights statute separate from IDEA. A public-school student may qualify for a 504 plan without qualifying for special education under IDEA. The governing federal rules include:

- **34 CFR §104.33:** FAPE and education services designed to meet the individual educational needs of qualified students with disabilities as adequately as the needs of nondisabled students are met.
- **34 CFR §104.35:** evaluation and placement procedures; decisions must draw on information from a variety of sources and be made by a group knowledgeable about the student, evaluation data, and placement options.
- **34 CFR §104.36:** procedural safeguards.

A district should not make a student wait for a particular intervention period before considering an evaluation when disability is suspected. For IDEA referrals, current 19 TAC §89.1011 says a referral may be initiated at any time and a district may not require a student to undergo interventions for a fixed period before referral or a full individual and initial evaluation.

## Texas-specific process anchors

- **TEC §29.005:** requires an IEP committee before enrollment in special education and requires the committee to develop the IEP by agreement or an agency-provided alternate method; majority vote may not be used.
- **19 TAC §89.1050:** establishes the ARD committee and its responsibilities. The October 2026 rule text includes notice and meeting procedures, including five-school-day notice provisions unless a shorter period is agreed.
- **19 TAC §89.1055:** requires the IEP to comply with federal IEP-content rules, annual review, PLAAFP information, measurable annual goals, and applicable Texas requirements.
- **TEC §26.008:** requires districts to provide parents information about options and requirements for assistance to students with learning difficulties or who may need special education, including the right to request an IDEA evaluation or Section 504 aids, accommodations, or services.
- **19 TAC §101.3013:** permits testing accommodations unless they would invalidate a test; decisions must consider student needs and accommodations routinely received in classroom instruction. For students receiving special education, the ARD committee determines and documents allowable accommodations in the IEP.

## Classroom-to-assessment safeguard

The safest workflow is:

1. Identify the barrier and the support, not merely a disability label.
2. Use the support during ordinary instruction and classroom testing early enough to establish whether it is effective.
3. Document the support in the IEP, 504 plan, or other authorized school documentation as applicable.
4. Check the current TEA policy document for the specific assessment and support.
5. Confirm the required decision-maker, eligibility criteria, documentation, TIDE entry, and TEA-approval deadline.
6. Practice the online tool or procedure before testing.
7. Record implementation and report failures promptly; do not improvise a prohibited accommodation on test day.

## Caveats for knowledgebase users

- A disability diagnosis alone does not determine a particular accommodation; the plan must connect the support to an educational or access need.
- An IEP or 504 plan does not guarantee every classroom support will be allowable on every state assessment.
- Local district procedures can add operational steps, but cannot replace or contradict applicable federal law, Texas law/rule, or TEA assessment policy.
- Assessment policies change by administration and school year. Re-check TEA’s current DCCR accommodation pages and guide before relying on a support.
- Do not put student names, IDs, dates of birth, medical records, or other personally identifying information in this knowledgebase.
