# Texas state assessments and accommodations

> **Current-year note (November 12, 2026).** The **2026–27 school year is the final full year of STAAR**; TEA notes certain end-of-course administrations continue through December 2027. House Bill 8 replaces STAAR with the Student Success Tool (SST) beginning in **2027–28**. Do not treat future SST accommodations, administration procedures, or deadlines as final unless TEA has published them.

## The rule that matters most

A classroom accommodation is not automatically a STAAR accommodation. For STAAR, use the current TEA assessment policy for the specific administration, subject, and grade/course. The **2026–2027 STAAR Accommodations Educator Guide**, updated October 20, 2026, explains that accommodations are intended to remove barriers in content, presentation, or response modes without changing the knowledge and skills being assessed.

The TEA framework distinguishes:

1. **Accessibility features**, available under current policy and administration procedures, including applicable routine-use provisions;
2. **Locally approved designated supports**, which require the appropriate local decision and documentation; and
3. **Designated supports requiring TEA approval**, which require an application/approval process and applicable deadlines.

Use the current guide and DCCR policy pages, together with applicable law and rules, to determine whether a support is allowed; do not rely on a generic list, an old IEP template, or a vendor’s claim.

## Eligibility and routine-use safeguards

For an IEP student, the ARD committee determines the assessment participation and needed supports under IDEA, Texas special-education rules, and current TEA policy. For a Section 504 student, the district’s 504 process must make an individualized decision consistent with 34 CFR Part 104 and the current TEA policy. The plan should identify the support, conditions, and assessment(s) to which it applies.

TEA’s guide emphasizes that a student should use an accommodation routinely during classroom instruction and testing and have an opportunity to practice it before the operational assessment. The support should be effective for the student and should not invalidate the skill or construct the test is designed to measure.

Operational checks:

- Confirm the support is allowed for the exact test, subject, grade/course, and administration mode.
- Confirm the correct local decision-maker and whether TEA approval is required.
- Confirm documentation and TIDE entry requirements with the district/campus testing coordinator.
- Practice online embedded tools and any non-embedded procedure on the TEA/Cambium practice site.
- Confirm whether a paper or special administration is needed when an online support cannot meet the student’s access need.
- Follow the current test administrator manual and security rules; do not provide coaching, clarification of test content, or an otherwise prohibited aid.

## Common category traps

- **Extended time** is not one universal permission. Its conditions, timing, and whether it is an approved support depend on the current TEA policy and test.
- **Text-to-speech, oral administration, or content read aloud** can be limited by subject, item type, language, and whether reading is the construct being measured.
- **Calculation aids, dictionaries, spelling tools, and reference materials** are controlled by current subject-specific policy; classroom availability does not establish STAAR availability.
- **Human scribes, speech-to-text, complex assistive technology, or nonstandard response modes** may require special procedures, TEA approval, or a special administration.
- A support may be allowable for one student and not another if the eligibility criteria, plan documentation, or implementation conditions differ.

See [TEA’s transition update](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/overview-house-bill-8) and [2027–28 assessment calendar](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/school-year-2027-28-student-assessment-testing-calendar).

## 2026–27 transition facts

- TEA identifies 2026–27 as the last full STAAR year.
- TEA’s HB 8 updates describe SST as a shorter, instructionally supportive program with beginning-of-year (BOY), middle-of-year (MOY), and end-of-year (EOY) assessment components. Some BOY/MOY requirements and approved alternatives are being implemented through separate TEA guidance.
- TEA has published a Commissioner’s List of approved alternative BOY/MOY instruments for statewide use beginning in 2027–28. A school system remains responsible for ensuring the instrument it uses is on the applicable approved list at administration.

These transition facts do **not** answer what accommodations will be available on SST. Maintain a separate future-policy update when TEA publishes final SST accessibility and accommodation guidance.

## Escalation path

1. Ask the campus testing coordinator to identify the exact TEA policy section and decision category.
2. Ask the special-education case manager/ARD chair or 504 coordinator to reconcile the assessment support with the student’s plan.
3. If TEA approval or a special administration is implicated, use the current DCCR process and deadline; do not wait until the testing session.
4. Document any missed or incorrectly delivered support and its impact, then use district and TEA complaint/appeal channels as appropriate.
