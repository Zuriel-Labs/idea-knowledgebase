# Texas classroom accommodations

> **Status:** General guidance checked November 12, 2026. This page describes legal and implementation anchors; it does not prescribe a student’s support. The student’s ARD/IEP or 504 team makes individualized decisions.

## What an accommodation should do

An accommodation removes or reduces an access barrier while preserving the intended instructional target. Examples may include:

- accessible formats, enlarged or otherwise adapted presentation, or text-to-speech when appropriate;
- explicit directions, chunking, repetition, or checks for understanding;
- preferential or reduced-distraction seating;
- planned breaks, flexible response mode, or extended time;
- assistive technology, speech-to-text, or alternate access to materials;
- organization, note-taking, or executive-function supports; and
- behavior, communication, sensory, or health-related supports needed for access.

These are examples, not an entitlement list. A support may be an accommodation in one context and a modification or an invalid test aid in another. The plan should specify the actual tool/procedure and its limits.

## IEP / ARD requirements

The current Texas rule is **19 TAC §89.1055**. It requires the IEP to comply with 34 CFR §§300.320 and 300.324 and to include applicable Texas requirements. The rule requires at least annual review (no later than 365 days after the prior annual review) and a PLAAFP describing how disability affects involvement and progress in the general curriculum, strengths, critical needs, and baseline data.

The federal IEP rule, **34 CFR §300.320(a)(4)**, requires the IEP to state the special education, related services, supplementary aids and services, program modifications, and supports for school personnel that will be provided, plus an explanation of the extent, if any, of nonparticipation with nondisabled students. This is the legal reason a plan should identify supports with enough specificity for a classroom teacher to implement them.

The ARD committee is governed in Texas by **19 TAC §89.1050** and **TEC §29.005**. A parent may request an ARD meeting; under §89.1050(e), the district must schedule and convene the meeting under the rule or provide written reasons for refusing within five school days.

## Section 504 plans

Section 504 is governed for federally assisted education programs by **34 CFR Part 104**, especially §§104.33–104.36. A 504 team evaluates and places a qualified student using information from multiple sources and a group knowledgeable about the student, evaluation data, and placement options (§104.35). The plan should describe the aids, services, and procedures needed for equal access and identify who will implement and monitor them.

IDEA and Section 504 are related but not identical. A student can have a 504 plan without an IEP; a student with an IEP is also protected against disability discrimination. District staff should follow the governing plan and applicable procedural safeguards rather than treating one form as a substitute for the other.

## Documentation pattern

For each support, capture:

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Barrier/need | Observable access problem and relevant data, not just a diagnosis |
| Support | Exact accommodation, tool, or procedure |
| Conditions | When, where, for which tasks/subjects, and any exclusions |
| Dosage | Frequency, duration, timing, or trigger |
| Responsible staff | Role(s) responsible for delivery and backup |
| Student use | Whether the student can initiate/use it independently |
| Effectiveness | Work samples, observations, grades, behavior/engagement, or assessment data |
| Review | Date/trigger for revisiting the decision |

Avoid vague language such as “extra help,” “preferential treatment,” or “extended time” without defining the amount and conditions. If staffing or technology prevents delivery of a required support, escalate promptly; resource constraints do not authorize staff to disregard an applicable plan.

## Instructional safeguards

1. **Teach and practice the support.** A student should not first encounter a device, oral administration routine, calculator aid, or unusual response process during a high-stakes test.
2. **Preserve the construct.** The support should not supply the answer or remove the skill the task is intended to measure.
3. **Use data.** Compare performance with and without the support where appropriate; revise when it is ineffective, stigmatizing, unsafe, or no longer needed.
4. **Coordinate across settings.** Communicate the operational plan to general-education, special-education, related-service, substitute, and testing staff while limiting disclosure to what each staff member needs to implement the plan.
5. **Plan transitions.** Confirm supports when schedules, campuses, teachers, devices, or assessment administrations change.
6. **Escalate missed services.** Record the date, class/task, impact, and corrective action; notify the case manager or 504 coordinator promptly.

## Texas dyslexia caveat

When a student is identified with dyslexia or a related disorder in special education, current 19 TAC §89.1040 and §89.1055 interact with **19 TAC §74.28** and the Texas Dyslexia Handbook. Do not treat a generic accommodation list as a substitute for the dyslexia-related evaluation, ARD documentation, instructional requirements, or current TEA handbook.

## What this page does not decide

This page does not determine eligibility, placement, grading, graduation, discipline, compensatory services, or whether a particular support is allowed on STAAR. Those decisions require the applicable team, current law/rule, and current TEA assessment policy.
